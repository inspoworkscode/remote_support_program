Remote support and job tracking, in one program

Made for businesses that look after other people's computers and jobs — IT companies first. Connect to your customers' computers, track every job from booking in to invoice, and give your customers a small app with your name on it. This guide shows everything it does, with real screenshots.

theremotesupportprogram.com
What it is

One program instead of three

Most small IT companies, and the other businesses doing the same kind of work, pay for a remote control tool, a separate job card system, and something else again for chat. The Remote Support Program does all of it, in one place, at one price — and every job you do on a computer is written down for you.

It has three parts

For your technicians

The console

A Windows program. Every customer's computers in one list, green when they are on. Double-click one to see its screen and fix it. Tickets, chat and the knowledge base are a click away.

For your office

The web portal

Runs in any browser, also on a phone. Book jobs in, follow them step by step, quote, invoice, manage clients, people and your branding.

For your customers

The customer app

A small app with your logo. One click asks you for help. Installed, it lets you in when they allow it, or when they are away if they switch that on.

Who it is for

  • IT companies, with a workshop, call outs and remote support.
  • Software and point-of-sale suppliers, in-house IT teams, repair shops and call-out businesses.
  • Five people on jobs, plus the owner; more on request.
  • Reaching your own computers, at a branch or a shop: the Basic Version for Remote Workers, R50 a month, or the Free Version for one (page ).

Why it is different

  • Built from the ground up. The remote control, the picture, the protocol and the portal are all our own work. Nothing is licensed from anyone else.
  • Your brand, not ours. Your customers see your name, logo and colours.
  • The paperwork does itself. Every remote session becomes a ticket, with the time it took.
  • Made in South Africa, hosted in South Africa, priced in rand.

What you need

Windows 10 or 11 for the console and for your customers' computers. Any web browser for the portal. A phone with an authenticator app (Microsoft or Google Authenticator) for signing in. Nothing to host or maintain yourself.

Remote control

See it and fix it, as if you were there

Double-click a computer and you are on it. The picture is sharp and quick on a good line, and steps itself down on a poor one rather than freezing.

A session window. Bottom left, a green lock says the session is encrypted end to end. The customer's knowledge base is one click away.

  • A video-quality picture, using the graphics card where there is one. A scrolling page needs a small fraction of the data older methods use, and the customer's computer barely notices.
  • Adapts to the connection: on a slow line it lowers the quality rather than stalling, and a still screen sharpens itself within two seconds.
  • The customer's real mouse pointer, in its real shape.
  • Every screen: pick the monitor, go full screen, or pop the picture out.
  • Admin (UAC) prompts and the Ctrl+Alt+Del screen can be seen and clicked.
  • Windows shortcuts go to their computer: the Windows key, Alt+Tab, Ctrl+Shift+Esc and the rest.

The System menu.

  • Restart the computer and the session waits, then reconnects by itself. A dropped connection recovers on its own too.
  • Two computers at once, one on each of your monitors. Copy files on one and paste on the other: they go straight between the two computers, never through yours.
  • Several technicians can join the same session.
  • Wake up a computer that is switched off, from the right-click menu.

During a session

Everything you need, without a cluttered screen

The tools live in one Tools menu. Each opens as its own small window you can move anywhere, so the customer's screen stays big.

  • Copy and paste, both ways: text, files and whole folders, as if the two computers were one.
  • Drag and drop files onto their screen, or off it onto yours.
  • Files: browse their computer, send and fetch files.
  • Scripts: run PowerShell on their computer and watch the result.
  • Sound: says in plain English why there is no sound, and plays a test tone.
  • Listen to the computer, and talk to the customer. They are asked each time before their microphone is used.
  • Record this session, saved as a video on the ticket.
  • Privacy screen: their monitor goes black while you work.
  • Print on my printer: their computer gets a printer with your name on it, and whatever they print comes out on yours.
  • Print a file on their printer: send a document and it prints at their end.
  • Get outside help: bring somebody from outside your company into this session with a code (page ).

On an attended computer, the person there is asked first, by name, and presses Allow.

Everything is written down for you

Every remote session gets its own ticket. Who connected, to which computer, for how long, files sent, scripts run and text copied (only its size, never the words, because clipboards often hold passwords) all go onto it by themselves. Reconnect within 30 minutes and it carries on the same ticket.

Outside help

When somebody else needs to help

Sometimes a customer needs their accounting software's supplier on their computer, or you need a specialist. Instead of a separate remote tool, that person comes in with a one-time code, and you stay in charge.

A technician asks for help: a code for this session, and a message to copy or send by WhatsApp or email.

Or the customer makes a code in your app and sends it.

Your technician asks

  • In a session, Tools → Get outside help. The code works once, for ten minutes, for that session only.
  • The technician lets them in, with files only if they tick it. The person at the computer is told.
  • They leave when your technicians do, and any technician can remove them.

Your customer asks

  • From your app's menu: Let someone else connect. The customer is always asked to Allow the person, by name.
  • You are told straight away in the console, and can watch or end it.
  • Off unless you switch it on, for your company and then for any single computer.

The other person types the code in a web browser. No account, nothing to install.

  • Screen and control only. No scripts, clipboard or sound for an outsider; files only when ticked.
  • They can use a browser, a small program for Windows, or, if their IT company uses our program too, their own console.
  • Everybody sees who they are and which company they say they are from.

Security

Access to your customers' computers, properly guarded

This software reaches deep into your customers' computers, so signing in and connecting are guarded seriously, and an outside reviewer has tested it.

Setting up two-factor sign-in with an authenticator app.

Recovery codes, in case a phone is lost.

Signing in elsewhere signs the other place out.

  • End-to-end encryption: a session is sealed between your console and the customer's computer. Our server passes it along but cannot see it. The console shows a green lock, and warns you if a computer is not the one it knew.
  • Two-factor sign-in for everyone, with recovery codes. A browser you trust asks for the code once a week.
  • One sign-in per person at a time. Remove someone, or change their password or role, and they are signed out at once.
  • Lockout after repeated wrong passwords, without letting a stranger lock you out of your own computer.
  • The customer decides: they allow each attended session, hold the unattended switch, and can set an unattended password.
  • A reinstalled computer waits for the office: until a supervisor confirms it, nobody gets in unattended.
  • Signed apps: every program carries the signature of The Remote Support Program (Pty) Ltd, so Windows shows who made it.
  • Clipboard contents are never logged, and the office administrator has no remote access at all.
  • Each company's data is kept apart, and addresses never reveal anything about other companies.

Tested from the outside

In October 2026 an independent security reviewer tested the platform from the outside. No way into another company's data was found. Every point raised was checked, and the ones that needed it were fixed before go-live.

Some people just need to reach their own computers — the one at the office while they work from home, or a branch or a shop — with nobody there, or with somebody there to click Allow. The Basic Version for Remote Workers does that for R50 a month after a 14-day free trial; for one computer, the Free Version does it for nothing.

What it has

  • One person, who is both the owner and the technician.
  • Three computers they or their own company own, or whose owner lets them reach them — and more at R10 a month each, up to 20, added on Billing.
  • Unattended or attended: with the computer's password, chosen when the app is installed, nobody needs to be there; without one, the person there is asked to allow you in.
  • Remote control, files, copy and paste, and printing both ways.
  • Two-factor sign-in and end-to-end encryption, the same as the full version.

What it leaves out

  • No support for customers: it is for your own computers only.
  • No tickets, clients, knowledge base, chat or team.
  • No nag screens. Cancel any time, or move down to the Free Version: one computer, unattended, nothing to pay.
  • Upgrade in place: the same account, computers and saved passwords become the full version, the full price taking over from the next payment.

Everyone at the computer knows: while someone is connected, a bar across the top of every screen says who it is. It cannot be closed, and the mouse goes straight through it.

The Basic console: just their own computers, and an Upgrade button.

Installing on a computer: the password is chosen here — or left blank, to be asked each time.
