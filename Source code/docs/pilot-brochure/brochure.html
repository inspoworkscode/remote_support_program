1Open your pilot link

We email you a link that starts with theremotesupportprogram.com/pilot/. It is yours alone: it works once, for 30 days. Open it on a computer rather than a phone; there is some reading.

First the terms. The pilot agreement comes first; scroll through, tick the box at the bottom, press Continue.

Then your company. Your company name and email are filled in from the link.

  1. Read the terms and tick I have read these terms and I accept them on behalf of my company. Press Continue.
  2. Check the company name. It appears on everything your customers see, so spell it the way you want it shown. (You can change it later on Branding.)
  3. Your name, contact number, work email and a password of 8 characters or more. The email is your login. You are the owner, and because you can also work tickets and connect to computers, you use one of your five seats.
  4. Primary and accent colours: click each square and pick your company's colours. The primary colour is for banners, the accent for buttons. Easy to change later.
  5. Ticket email address (optional): the address your customers email for support, such as support@yourcompany.co.za. You finish setting it up later (step 9).
  6. Press Create my account. Your account is ready at once.
Time zone: South Africa is filled in for you. Every time in the portal is shown in your company's time, never the server's.

8Connect for the first time

With the console open and the app on a customer's computer, you are ready. Try it first on a computer in your own office.

The console: computers grouped by client, green when they are on. Requests for help pop up at the top.

You start it

  1. Double-click the computer (or right-click, Connect).
  2. Say in a few words what it is about. That becomes the ticket's subject.
  3. On an attended computer the person there presses Allow. Unattended, you are straight in (with the password, if one is set).
  4. Work as if you were there. Tools at the top has files, chat, scripts, printing and the rest.
  5. Close the window when you are done.

They ask for help

  1. The customer presses Request Support in their app and says what is wrong.
  2. It rings softly in every technician's console, with their words and, if they ticked it, a picture of their screen.
  3. Press Accept and you are connected.
The ticket writes itself. Every session gets its own ticket with who, which computer, how long, files sent and scripts run. Reconnecting within 30 minutes carries on the same ticket without asking again.

New · Remote control and your customers

Sharper, two at once, and a better picture of the problem

A picture that never stays blurry

On some computers the screen arrived blocky and only slowly sharpened. Now a still screen is sharpened within two seconds of anything changing, and stays sharp.

Two computers at once

  • Open two sessions side by side, one on each of your monitors. Two is the most, so nobody spreads themselves thin; reconnecting to one you have open never counts.
  • Copy on one, paste on the other. Copy files on the first computer, click into the second and paste. The files go straight between the two computers through our server, sealed, never through your PC. Moving a client's data to their new computer no longer waits on your internet line.

Signed apps

Every program is now signed by The Remote Support Program (Pty) Ltd. Windows names us as the verified publisher instead of "Unknown publisher", and antivirus programs can see exactly who made it.

Asking for help: their name is remembered, and they can take a new picture of the window they mean.

  • The app remembers the customer's name, so they type it once.
  • Customers often minimise everything to find our icon on the desktop, so the picture showed only the desktop. Now it says so, and Take a new picture lets them bring up the window first.

During a session

Everything you need, without a cluttered screen

The tools live in one Tools menu. Each opens as its own small window you can move anywhere, so the customer's screen stays big.

The Tools menu in a session.

  • Copy and paste, both ways: text, files and whole folders.
  • Drag and drop files onto their screen, or off it onto yours.
  • Files: browse their computer, send and fetch files.
  • Scripts: run PowerShell on their machine and watch the result.
  • Sound: says in plain English why there is no sound, and plays a test tone.
  • Listen to the computer, and talk to the customer. They are asked each time before their microphone is used.
  • Record this session, saved as a video on the ticket.
  • Privacy screen: their monitor goes black while you work.
  • Print on my printer: their computer gets a printer with your name on it. Whatever they print comes out on yours.
  • Print a file on their printer: send a document and it prints at their end.

Everything is written down for you

Every remote session gets its own ticket. Files sent, text copied (only its size, never the words, because clipboards often hold passwords), scripts run and how long it took all go onto it automatically.

Outside help

When someone else needs to help your customer

Sometimes your customer needs their accountant, or a software supplier, on their computer. Instead of a separate remote tool, they let that person in with a one-time code. You stay in the picture.

The customer makes a code in your app and reads it out.

The other person types the code in a web browser. No account and nothing to install.

  • A nine-digit code that works once, for ten minutes.
  • The customer is always asked to allow the person, by name, even on a computer that is normally unattended.
  • You are told straight away, in the console. You can watch the session or end it.
  • The other person can use a browser, or a small program for Windows. If they use our program at their own IT company, they connect with that.
  • Screen and control only. No scripts, no clipboard, no sound.
  • Files only if the customer ticks it when they allow the person.
  • Off unless you switch it on: for your whole company, and then on or off for any single computer.
  • Never unattended. It only works while someone is at the computer.
